The quarter's core business numbers are unambiguous: record deliveries, revenue crossing $100 billion on a trailing-twelve-month basis for the first time, and FSD subscriptions growing faster than the fleet itself. Net income, meanwhile, fell sharply and missed estimates even as revenue beat — the AI buildout is being funded by the car business, not yet by the robotaxi business.

But the number getting the most airtime on the call is cumulative paid Robotaxi miles, presented as a climbing line on Tesla's own shareholder chart. We wanted to know what that line looks like once you stop looking at it cumulatively.

A cumulative chart only goes one direction

That's the whole reason to put one in front of investors — it always slopes up, no matter what happened quarter to quarter. The honest read is to difference the quarter-end totals. Do that with Tesla's own numbers, and the growth story changes.

What the mile count doesn't say about safety

The most recent public safety tally, compiled through mid-January 2026, put Tesla's Austin Robotaxi service at 14 crashes across roughly 800,000 cumulative paid miles — about one incident every 57,000 miles. Tesla's own Vehicle Safety Report benchmarks the average US driver at one minor collision per 229,000 miles, which puts the robotaxi fleet at roughly four times that rate, by Tesla's own yardstick. FSD software is also currently under an active NHTSA probe.

"If we injure even one person, it will be worldwide headline news, and regulators will immediately clamp down on our activities."

— Elon Musk, Q2 2026 earnings call

It's the right instinct, and it's also why the pace of the rollout — not just the mileage total — is the number worth tracking next quarter.

ROBUST

The core auto business funding the bet

Record deliveries beating estimates by roughly 74,000 units, revenue growth accelerating, and an FSD subscriber base compounding at 56% year over year. Whatever happens with Robotaxi, the balance sheet behind it is real.

FRAGILE

The robotaxi growth narrative, specifically

The cumulative chart is doing a lot of narrative work that the quarter-over-quarter numbers don't back up yet. Flat mileage growth, a mid-quarter slowdown, a safety rate still trailing Tesla's own driver benchmark, and an open federal probe are all live at once.

DEAD

"Half the US mainland by end of 2025"

Quietly downgraded across three straight guidance cycles, most recently to seven new cities in the first half of 2026. Worth remembering the next time a coverage timeline gets announced on a call.

Why we're watching the data trail, not just the mile count

Every one of those paid miles is also a data event — GPS trace, cabin footage, a payment record. Right now, the only public account of it is one chart a company draws for its own shareholder deck, and outside readers have to reverse-engineer the real trend by hand, pixel by pixel, the way we just did above.
